Constraints changes vs time

 

1. Overview

The initial process constraints (maximum rates, uptime, pressures...) are entered on the network nodes, see Network modelling. They can then change during the forecast in two ways:

•by executing an opportunity that changes a constraint (for example a debottlenecking project), see Opportunities;

•by entering the change directly in the "Constraints" table, at a fixed date or at a date relative to an opportunity.

Use the "Constraints" table for seasonal or recurrent capacity changes, temporary limits, uptime assumptions for a well, a manifold or the whole field, and to keep a well online whatever the optimiser decides ("Override solver").

2. Open the table

1.Select the scenario on the main window.

2.Click "Constraints vs time" in the "Expertise" group of the main ribbon.

The window title is "Constraints". It works like the "Shutdowns" table: "Save", "Add", "Delete selected rows", "Delete All rows", "Help", "Filter by project:" and "Filter Node:" (see Shutdowns).

Example of constraint changes: a temporary injection limit, a seasonal gas capacity repeated every 12 months, and a reduced uptime for a new well.

3. Columns

Column

Meaning

"Project"

The project of the change. Changes of a project set to "No" in "Settings" > "To be included in Forecast" are ignored.

"Node"

The node or well. Also "All new Wells" (applies to every new well) and "Field" (to change the field uptime over time).

"Constraint"

The type of constraint, see section 4.

"Value"

The new value, in the unit of the constraint (rates and pressures in the asset's units; uptime as a fraction).

"Constraint Start_Date" / "Constraint End_Date"

Fixed dates of the change. With no end date and no end opportunity, the change is permanent (until the end of the forecast).

"Start constraint when Opportunity", "Reference date", "Number of days before"

Relative start date, exactly as for shutdowns: "start" or "end" of an opportunity, minus a number of days (negative = after).

"End constraint when Opportunity", "Reference date ", "Number of days before "

Relative end date.

"Apply every x days" / "Apply every x months" / "Apply recurrent constraint until"

Repeats the change (seasonal capacity, recurrent uptime).

"CAPEX", "OPEX", "OPEX unit", "Group cost to Node"

Costs of the change, see Economical screener.

"Comment", "Linked to Opportunity"

Free text; opportunity that created the row.

4. Constraint types

4.1 Always available

Constraint

Effect

"Oil", "Water", "Gas", "Gas-lift", "Total Gas", "Liquid"

Maximum rate through the node. "Total Gas" is produced gas plus gas-lift.

"Water Injection", "Gas Injection"

Maximum injection rate through the node or injector.

"Uptime"

Uptime fraction of the well, manifold or field (0 to 1). A value above 1 on a well is treated as an incremental gain (the extra production is booked to the project).

"Oil storage"

Oil storage limit of a storage node.

"Override solver"

Enter 1 to stop the optimiser from shutting in or choking back the well (see section 6).

4.2 With "Decline curves" (Settings)

Constraint

Effect

"Fixed Liquid rate"

Forces the well to produce this liquid rate (its potential is scaled to it). The gain is booked to the project.

"Add Reserves to remaining"

Adds a fraction of the well's remaining reserves (for example 0.2 = +20 %) from that date.

"Fixed Gas-lift rate"

Forces the gas-lift rate of the well.

4.3 With "Material Balance" or "Simulation" (Settings)

Constraint

Effect

"Min Pressure", "Max Pressure"

Pressure limit at a node, in the asset's pressure unit (gauge).

"THP" (well only)

Tubing head pressure of the well.

"Min FBHP", "Max Drawdown", "Max IBHP", "PI" (well only)

Minimum flowing bottom-hole pressure, maximum drawdown, maximum injection bottom-hole pressure, productivity index.

Other entries appear only when "Pre/Post processor for GAP" or "3D Simulation model" is ticked in Settings (for example "VLP", "Fixed dP", "THP target", "BHP target").

1.The maximum "Water Injection" rate of injector A1-I1 is set to 1000 bwpd between 28/10/2023 and 12/11/2023.

6. Override solver

At every time step the optimiser may shut in or choke back wells to honour the process constraints, according to the objective function (see Settings). A row with "Constraint" = "Override solver" and "Value" = 1 forces the well to stay open at its full uptime, even if the optimiser would close it to optimise the hydrocarbon production. It only applies while the well is open and has no rate constraint of its own. No losses are booked for an override.

7. Uptime

The uptime of a well at each time step is: Field uptime × Manifold uptime × Well uptime. The field uptime is set in Settings ("Field uptime (fraction) :") and can be changed over time with "Node" = "Field" in this table. Manifold and well uptimes are set in this table. A manifold is the node directly downstream of a producer, or directly upstream of an injector.

The production of a well is its potential × its choke opening, and the choke opening can never exceed the well's total uptime. After the optimiser has solved the network, the choke opening can be lower than the uptime (the well is constrained), or 0 if a node downstream is closed.

8. When the changes are applied

•Rate, pressure and other process constraints are applied only if "Surface Constraints" is ticked in Settings.

•"Uptime" rows are applied only if "Uptime" is ticked.

•"Override solver", "Fixed Liquid rate" and "Fixed Gas-lift rate" rows are always applied.

Check the input on the timeline of the main window, where every change of constraint is shown.
