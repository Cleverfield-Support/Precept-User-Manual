Forecasting with Material Balance (MBAL)

 

1. Overview — what Material Balance is, and how it differs from DCA

Precept can generate each well's potential (its unconstrained deliverability at every forecast time step) in one of two ways:

•Decline Curves (DCA) — the potential is read from a fitted decline curve. It extrapolates observed history and does not "know" about reservoir pressure, aquifer support, or injection.

•Material Balance (MBAL) — the potential is computed from physics. Each reservoir is modelled as a tank whose pressure falls as fluids are produced and rises as fluids (or aquifer water) come in. That reservoir pressure is turned into a well flow rate through an inflow model (IPR) and a lift curve (VLP).

Because MBAL is pressure-driven, the forecast responds to depletion, aquifer influx, and injection / voidage replacement — things DCA cannot represent. Typical reasons to use MBAL instead of DCA:

•You want the forecast to react to a pressure-maintenance / water- or gas-injection strategy (voidage replacement).

•You are forecasting a green field or a new well with little or no production history to fit a decline curve.

•You want producer GOR / water-cut to evolve from reservoir physics (relative permeability, gas coming out of solution) rather than from a hand-drawn trend.

Both methods feed the same network solver: once potentials exist (from DCA or MBAL), Precept runs its linear solver at each time step to apply process/facility constraints and decide which wells are choked back or shut in. MBAL is therefore a potential generator in front of the same optimisation — not a separate forecasting engine.

2. Choosing the method — the "Decline" setting

The forecast method is controlled by the "Decline" setting in the Settings section:

•"Decline curves" → the scenario forecasts with DCA.

•"Material Balance" → the scenario forecasts with MBAL.

•"Simulation" → the network forecast runs with a reservoir simulator (OPM Flow) in the loop instead of the material-balance tanks. At each time step Precept solves the network, sends each well its control (BHP, rate or shut-in), runs the simulator for that step only, and uses the simulator's PI, water-cut and GOR as each well's inflow, iterating until the rates agree. It needs a deterministic run with the Precept optimiser, oilfield (psig) units, and a .DATA deck generated with the 3D model builder.

(The setting can also be changed conversationally in Precept AI, e.g. "use material balance for this scenario".)

The method is chosen per scenario, so you can keep a DCA base case and an MBAL variant side by side and compare them.

3. The Material Balance section — reservoir inputs

Open the Material Balance section from the Main User Interface (in Precept AI: "open material balance data"). Data is entered per reservoir — select the reservoir in the "Reservoir" list (the reservoirs of the scenario's wells), fill in its properties, and click "Save Reservoir properties" (or Ctrl+S). The window has two tabs: "Reservoir properties" (sections 3.1 to 3.5) and "PVT" (section 6).

The ribbon of the Material Balance window gives access to the related tools:

•Well Inflow control — the per-well inflow data (inflow model, c and n, perforated interval, wellbore radius, skin).

•Well completion and VLP — opens Set Well Geometry (section 4) to build the lift curves.

•VLP/IPR check at start forecast — a nodal check of each producer on the first forecast day (section 5.3).

•Import PVT table — paste a PVT table (e.g. from Prosper) instead of generating it.

•Nodal Analysis (see Nodal analysis and well models), and Help (this page).

3.1 Tank (reservoir) properties

Input

Reservoir temperature

Initial solution gas–oil ratio (scf/bbl).

Stock-tank oil originally in place (MMstb) — the oil tank size.

Original gas in place (MMscf) — the gas tank size.

Porosity

Fraction.

Connate Sw

Connate/irreducible water saturation (fraction). Also constrains the relative-permeability curves (see below).

Rock compressibility

1/psi.

The tank pressure at the next step is computed from the volumetric balance of everything produced and injected (and any aquifer influx) over the step — so over-injection is legitimate: injecting more than you produce lets a declining reservoir recover pressure (voidage replacement ratio, VRR, greater than 1 is physically meaningful in MBAL).

3.2 Aquifer support — Fetkovich aquifer

An optional Fetkovich aquifer provides pressure support from water influx:

•Productivity Index (RB/day/psi) — how readily the aquifer feeds the reservoir.

•Max influx capacity (MMstb) — the total water the aquifer can supply.

Leave the aquifer inactive for a purely depletion / injection-supported reservoir.

3.3 Relative permeabilities

Enter the relative-permeability curves that govern how water-cut and GOR (or WGR) evolve as saturations change during depletion:

•Oil reservoir — two tables and charts: water/oil (Sw, Krw, Kro) and gas/oil (Sg, Krg, Kro).

•Gas reservoir — one table and chart: water/gas (Sw, Krw, Krg).

Points can be dragged directly on the charts. Rule enforced by Precept: the Krw endpoint must equal the connate water saturation — the form warns if it does not.

3.4 Well inflow — Jones model and c and n

The inflow model turns the drawdown (reservoir pressure minus flowing bottom-hole pressure) into a rate. Reservoir-level inputs on this tab, for the Jones Well Inflow model:

•Permeability (md)

•kv/kh (vertical-to-horizontal permeability ratio)

The per-well data (perforated interval, wellbore radius, skin) come from Well Inflow control. The drainage area is derived from the tank size and shared between the active wells.

Gas producers and gas injectors can use either model, chosen per well in Well Inflow control:

•c and n (back-pressure equation): q = C × (Pr² − Pwf²)^n. This is the default for a gas well with no inflow model set (C = 0.0001, n = 1).

•Jones (gas form): A·q² + B·q = Pr² − Pwf², with A and B computed from the PVT, the reservoir properties, the per-well data and the gas/water relative permeabilities.

This is Precept's native, Prosper-independent IPR — no external nodal package is required.

3.5 Lift curves — the VLP folder

•VLP folder — a folder of lift-curve files (ECLIPSE VFPPROD .ecl format), one per well. The VLP converts a wellhead/manifold pressure and a rate into a flowing bottom-hole pressure, closing the loop between the reservoir (IPR) and the surface network. Pick the folder with the "..." button; it is saved immediately and is also where Set Well Geometry writes the generated VLPs.

You can generate these VLPs natively inside Precept — see Section 5.

4. The Well section (Set Well Geometry)

Each well's geometry and equipment are defined in Set Well Geometry, which has several tabs:

•Completion / Geometry — the well trajectory (measured depth vs TVD), tubing, and the X-mas tree / equipment depths. The datum for bottom-hole pressures is the deepest node TVD.

•PVT — the black-oil PVT used for this well/reservoir (see Section 6).

•ESP (only for ESP-lifted wells) — the electrical submersible pump: pump model (from the ESP catalogue), number of stages, and wear factor, with a live head-vs-rate preview. Precept models the pump stage-by-stage using affinity laws; running frequency comes from the well test, not this tab.

•VLP — generates the lift curves used by MBAL (Section 5).

5. VLP generation (native ECLIPSE VFPPROD export)

MBAL needs a lift curve (VLP) for every well. Precept generates these natively — no Prosper export is required. It writes standard ECLIPSE VFPPROD tables using its own pressure traverse; the "Correlation" list offers Beggs & Brill (default), Hagedorn-Brown and Gray, with F1/F2 tuning factors.

5.1 Oil producers

How it works (VLP tab in Set Well Geometry):

•A VFPPROD table is BHP tabulated against liquid rate. Liquid rate is the spine (the primary axis) — it is not optional.

•Pick the sensitivity axis you want (THP, water-cut/BSW, GOR, or gas-lift rate) and enter its values in the grid; the generator produces a BHP surface over rate × that sensitivity.

•Click Generate; a progress bar tracks the run. Non-converged points are written at the THP value and reported as a count.

Points worth knowing:

•Because the axis is liquid rate, a BSW of 100 % is a valid point (oil = liquid × (1 − wc), water = liquid × wc, so the two always sum to the liquid rate).

•Rate = 0 is a real, safe point (a static-gradient shut-in row).

•The datum is the deepest node TVD (matching the ECLIPSE/Prosper convention).

•Numbers are written culture-invariantly (decimal point, never a comma) so the files parse on any machine.

•Each well's table index must be unique within the folder.

•ESP wells have no VLP tab — their lift is the pump, so there is no meaningful gas-lift axis.

5.2 Gas producers

For a gas producer the VLP tab shows gas inputs instead of liquid, BSW and GOR:

•Gas rate is the spine of the table (VFPPROD with FLO = GAS).

•THP, WGR (water-gas ratio) and CGR (condensate-gas ratio) are the sensitivity axes; WGR and CGR are in stb/Mscf, or sm3/sm3 for metric assets.

•A gas-lift axis is added only when the well is gas-lifted.

Where the files go

When Set Well Geometry is opened from the Material Balance window, the generated file is saved automatically as <well>.vlp in the scenario's VLP folder (section 3.5) — no folder is asked. A save dialog appears only if no VLP folder is set yet, or if it no longer exists. Generation is refused if another well in that folder already uses the same table index.

5.3 VLP/IPR check at start of forecast

Before running a long forecast, check each producer's operating point. In the Material Balance window click "VLP/IPR check at start forecast", pick an oil or gas producer, choose THP or Drawdown control, enter a gas-lift rate if any, and click "Get rate at start of forecast".

The chart shows the IPR and VLP curves on the first forecast day and their intersection (the operating point); in Drawdown mode the VLP is not used. The panel lists the operating point (liquid, oil, water, gas, gas-lift, BHP, THP) and the inflow inputs used. A well that cannot flow here will not flow in the forecast either — fix its VLP, inflow data or constraints first.

6. PVT section

The PVT for Material Balance is entered/generated per reservoir and describes the pressure dependence of the fluid: Bo, Bg, Rs, and oil/gas/water viscosities as functions of pressure (standard black-oil PVT), plus the bubble point.

The PVT tab of the Material Balance window generates the table from black-oil correlations:

•Pb, Rs, Bo correlation: Glaso (default) or Standing.

•Inputs: oil gravity (°API), gas gravity (air = 1), solution GOR Rs, reservoir temperature, water salinity (ppm), and the table's minimum and maximum pressure. Temperature, Rs and maximum pressure are copied from the Reservoir properties tab.

•PVT match tuning (1 = untuned): multipliers on GOR/Pb, Bo and oil viscosity, to match lab data.

•Click "Generate", review the table (it includes Bw and water compressibility, computed from the salinity), then "Save PVT tables".

Alternatively, use "Import PVT table" on the ribbon to paste a table from Prosper or a lab report.

Notes:

•Precept stores pressures and temperatures in the display unit (e.g. barg / °C), but the correlations work internally in psia / °F — the conversion is handled for you.

•Gas volumes are mscf-consistent: Bg is in rb/mscf; gas injection and free-gas volumes are in mscf; Rs and producing GOR stay in scf/stb. The MBAL balance and the VLP/PVT chain assume these units throughout.

The same PVT feeds the tank balance, the inflow model, and the VLP generation, so it must be consistent across the three.

7. How the forecast uses MBAL at each time step

At every forecast step, for a reservoir in Material Balance mode, Precept:

1.Takes the current reservoir (tank) pressure for each MBAL reservoir.

2.Performs a manifold-coupled network solve: it gathers all the wells on a manifold, computes each well's inflow (IPR) against the current pressure and PVT, and balances the manifold so that the facilities lift curve converts the combined flow of all the wells into the shared manifold pressure — i.e. the back-pressure is computed from the summed flow, not assumed. This yields each well's potential.

3.Feeds those potentials into the linear solver, which allocates choke (0–100 % open per well) to maximise the objective under the surface/facility constraints — exactly as in a DCA forecast.

4.Applies the produced/injected volumes back to the tank balance to get the next step's reservoir pressure, and adds aquifer influx (if any). Injection into a Material Balance reservoir is not capped at the voidage (unlike a decline-curve reservoir): it is limited by each injector's injectivity and maximum injection BHP and by the network injection capacities, so injecting more than you produce raises the reservoir pressure.

Guard rails built in: a reservoir whose pressure has effectively died has its producers shut (their potential zeroed) while injectors keep going, so a dead reservoir gets little injection and cannot be over-flooded. This keeps the pressure-maintenance logic stable across multi-reservoir assets.

Gas reservoirs: each gas well's rate is limited by the most restrictive of its THP, manifold pressure, minimum BHP, maximum drawdown and gas-rate constraints. Its water-gas ratio comes from fractional flow, WGR = (Bg/Bw)·(krw/μw)/(krg/μg), so water production rises as the water saturation increases. Condensate is not modelled in material-balance mode (CGR = 0).

At the end of every run, the tank results of each step (pressure, saturations, Rs, GOR / WGR / CGR, oil, free-gas and water withdrawals, water and gas injection, net voidage, aquifer influx, effective compressibility) are saved to the database, so the Material Balance dashboard (section 10) can show them later.

8. Quick start — switching a scenario to Material Balance

1.In Settings, set "Decline" to "Material Balance".

2.In the Material Balance section, for each reservoir: enter the tank properties (initial pressure, STOOIP/OGIP, porosity, connate Sw, rock compressibility, thickness, Rs, temperature), the relative permeabilities, the Jones inflow parameters, an optional Fetkovich aquifer, and choose the VLP folder. Generate and save the PVT on the PVT tab. Click Save Reservoir properties.

3.In Well Inflow control, check each well's inflow model and data (c and n, or Jones with perforated interval, wellbore radius and skin).

4.In Set Well Geometry (Well completion and VLP), for each well: confirm the geometry/completion and, on the VLP tab, Generate the lift curve — it is saved straight into the VLP folder.

5.Run the VLP/IPR check at start forecast on a few producers to confirm they flow at sensible rates.

6.Set your injection / voidage strategy (initial constraints and constraints-vs-time as usual).

7.Run Forecast and review the results — reservoir pressure, rates, and voidage will now evolve from the physics.

9. History matching with probabilistic runs

A material-balance model is only as good as its history match. Precept history-matches by running the model over the production history many times with uncertain parameters, then comparing every run with the measured data in the Material Balance dashboard (section 10).

9.1 What you need

•The measured history: allocated production and injection (rates, BHP, THP) from Production monitoring, and the reservoir-pressure surveys of the wells (Reservoir pressures).

•A scenario in Material Balance mode whose forecast starts at the start of the field's production, so the run covers the whole history.

•The wells controlled as they were operated — on rate or on BHP, through the initial constraints and constraints vs time. History can be matched on rates or on BHP: the quantity you impose is the one you do not match.

9.2 Uncertain parameters

Open "Probabilistic distributions". When the scenario is in Material Balance mode two extra categories are offered:

•Material Balance — any reservoir input, named "<Reservoir> , <parameter>" (e.g. "Main , OGIP", "Main , Porosity", the aquifer size and productivity index, rock compressibility, PVT inputs).

•Well Inflow — each well's PI, or its c and n factors.

For each parameter enter a Min and a Max; the most likely value is the current deterministic input. Each run samples a value from a normal distribution centred on that value and truncated to [Min, Max]. Set the "Number of runs" in Settings, then click "Probabilistic".

Every run's sampled values and its step-by-step tank results are saved with the run, so they can be reviewed at any time. They are deleted with the run or the scenario. Ranges left over from a reservoir or a well that no longer exists in the scenario are removed automatically when Probabilistic distributions opens.

10. Material Balance dashboard

Click "Material Balance dashboard" on the Results ribbon (visible when the scenario's Decline setting is Material Balance). The dashboard compares the runs of the scenario with the measured history, on one screen, for one reservoir at a time.

10.1 Top bar

•Reservoir — the tank to show, when the model has several.

•Runs — Deterministic (the forecast run) or Probabilistic (all the realizations of the probabilistic run).

•Run — the run drawn in bold. By default the best-matched run is selected. You can also click any curve on any chart to select its run; hover over a curve to see which run it is.

•Well plots — the quantities drawn for every well (tick several): Gas rate, Water rate, Oil rate, Water injection, BSW, GOR, WGR, CGR, BHP, THP, Reservoir pressure.

•Runs table — a pop-up listing the runs ranked by match quality, with each run's sampled parameters. Click a row to select the run.

•Use this run — copies the selected run's sampled reservoir parameters into the scenario's Material Balance inputs (the deterministic case), after showing the old and new values for confirmation. Well inflow parameters are not copied.

10.2 Match quality

Each run is scored by a normalised RMS error (in %): the root-mean-square difference between the run and the measured points, divided by the mean measured value. It is computed separately for the reservoir pressure, the wells' BHP, gas rates and water rates, and the total is their average. The lower the better.

10.3 The charts

On every chart, the measured data are dots (pressure data: grey dots with a black border), every run is a faint line, and the selected run is a bold line.

Section

Charts

Reservoir — pressure and production

Reservoir pressure vs time (the surveys of all the reservoir's wells); rates; cumulative gas (or oil); cumulative water.

Material balance

P/Z vs cumulative gas (pressure vs cumulative oil for an oil tank); Havlena-Odeh F vs Et; Cole plot F/Et vs Gp; aquifer plot F/Et vs We/Et; drive mechanisms of the selected run; drive indices from the history; saturations; the PVT used (Z, Bg).

Wells

One chart per well of the reservoir, all wells at once, with the quantities ticked in Well plots (rates on the left axis, pressures and ratios on the right).

10.4 Reading the material-balance plots

•P/Z vs cumulative gas — a volumetric gas tank gives a straight line that reaches zero at the OGIP. Points bending above the line indicate pressure support (aquifer or injection).

•Havlena-Odeh — F (underground withdrawal, F = Gp·Bg + Wp·Bw − Winj·Bw) against the expansion Et (gas expansion Eg = Bg − Bgi plus rock and connate-water expansion). For a depletion drive the points lie on a straight line through the origin whose slope is the gas in place.

•Cole plot — F/Et against Gp. Flat for a depletion drive; rising points reveal aquifer support that the model may be missing.

•Aquifer plot — F/Et against We/Et using the selected run's aquifer influx. A good aquifer model gives a straight line with slope 1 and intercept G.

•Drive mechanisms — the share of the withdrawals supplied by each source over time: gas expansion, rock and water compressibility, aquifer influx, injection. On the history chart, anything the selected run's parameters cannot explain is shown as "Unexplained"; a large unexplained share means the tank size or support is wrong.

10.5 Typical workflow

1.Run a probabilistic run (e.g. 20 to 50 runs) with ranges on the main unknowns: OGIP / STOOIP, the aquifer, rock compressibility, the well inflow.

2.Open the Material Balance dashboard, Runs = Probabilistic. Open the Runs table to see which runs match best and which parameter values they share.

3.Check the best run on the charts, not only its score: the P/Z, Havlena-Odeh and Cole plots should agree with the drive the run assumes.

4.Narrow the ranges around the good runs and rerun if needed.

5.Click "Use this run" to keep its parameters in the scenario, then run the deterministic forecast from the matched model.
